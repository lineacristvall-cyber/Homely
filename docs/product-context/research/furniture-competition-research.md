# Furniture sourcing competition: evidence memo

Research date: 13 September 2026. Audience: founder considering a professional-interior-designer product first, with a possible later DIY offering. The product vision remains open; this memo does not authorize a build.

## Conclusion

There is substantial competition in visual furniture discovery, professional semantic search, constraint-sensitive specification, and the surrounding procurement workflow. A new product cannot claim differentiation merely through natural language, reference images, shortlists, explanations, firm memory, or uncertainty labels. Multiple companies already market those capabilities. Whether any delivers them reliably is untested here.

The supplied competitor-analysis document is useful as a candidate list, but its proposed differentiation is a hypothesis, not validated whitespace. Its inference that competing products establish customer demand is too strong: marketing pages show suppliers pursuing a problem, not willingness to pay, retention, or successful outcomes.

The clearest commercial pressure comes from free professional discovery and sourcing bundled into existing subscriptions. A paid specialist must establish incremental value after verification and handoff effort, not just produce attractive results faster.

All URLs below are official sources accessed 13 September 2026. Most are undated current product pages; an access date is not a publication date. Counts, usage, savings, AI performance and coverage are company claims. A working public landing page or trial link establishes an advertised offering, not that the underlying workflow succeeds. ## Direct competitors

### Vendora — closest professional-workflow concept, early access

Targets high-end studios and ateliers. It advertises brief/image/project-note input, three vetted candidates, refinement, pricing and lead times, private firm memory, vendor terms and presentation exports. Claims 34+ vendors and tens of thousands of products. Application-based early access; pricing is being finalized with founding firms, rather than a published subscription. The FAQ explicitly says Studio Designer and Fohlio integrations are planned, not live, despite a sync illustration earlier on the page. Customization outreach is drafted for user review and sending. Private atelier pricing and customization access are potentially meaningful but untested claims.

Implication: project context, a short list, vendor knowledge and remembered preferences are directly contested. Test whether lead-time evidence covers destination, exact variant and quantity, and distinguish known data from fresh confirmation.

Source: https://www.sourcevendora.com/ (undated; accessed 2026-09-13).

### Fringe Interior — free discovery, strongest commercial/contract orientation

Public search and category surfaces target designers, architects and FF&E procurement teams, especially hospitality, offices and restaurants. Advertises natural-language and image search, dimensions/material filters, product lists, Excel/CSV exports and clipping. Homepage counter reports 1,188,870 products and 3,015 brands; FAQ and designer page still say 500,000+ products and 3,000+ suppliers. Do not treat the differing counts as an independently confirmed inventory.

Its designer page explicitly says all search, image upload, shortlists and exports are free, without search/download limits, and funded by supplier partnerships. It also gives a commercial constraint example involving a fire rating. Therefore budget/constraint language and professional exports are already baseline claims.

Implication: strong zero-price comparison; test source accuracy, negative constraints and landed feasibility rather than assuming it is only superficial search. Supplier funding is not proof of biased rankings, but creates a question worth investigating.

Sources: https://www.fringeinterior.com/ ; https://www.fringeinterior.com/for-designers (undated; accessed 2026-09-13).

### SPEKD — semantic trade search plus an unresolved dealer-intelligence offering

Indexed official homepage content advertises 41,000+ trade products, 20 vendors, 34 product attributes, designer terminology, similar-item search and nightly price/image checks. Direct page extraction returned no body, so these specifics were available as official-domain search content rather than a full rendered product inspection.

A readable official “How It Works” page describes a different or related manufacturer-to-dealer product: approved spec sheets, source-cited configuration and lead-time answers, manufacturer review, caveats for stale data, escalation to a rep, and refusal outside scope. It states it is working with limited manufacturer design partners on first deployments, and offers a demo. No public price was verified. Current relationship between the search offering and dealer-intelligence offering is unresolved.

Implication: evidence and uncertainty are explicitly competitive promises. It is particularly important to test manufacturer data access and exact configurations, not assume an explanation layer is unique.

Sources: https://www.spekd.ai/ ; https://www.spekd.ai/how-it-works (undated; accessed 2026-09-13).

### Hanei — designer/client collaboration and aesthetic rationale

Targets interior designers and their clients. Advertises moodboard and client-note input, aesthetic matching across thousands of trade vendors, 10+ curated options, natural-language refinements, per-option client feedback, PDF/XLS/CSV exports, and visible aesthetic explanations. “Book demo” is the main access route; no public price or independently confirmed self-service access was established. Sample recommendation cards illustrate reasoning, rather than proving live reasoning quality. Its claim of saving five-plus hours weekly is not supported here by independent measurement.

Implication: compatibility-style explanations, client feedback and refinement are already marketed. Test whether the system produces defensible compatibility judgments instead of plausible descriptions. Coverage and viability performance remain unknown.

Source: https://www.tryhanei.app/ (undated; accessed 2026-09-13).

### Decato — room-level fit/budget workflow, narrower live store coverage

Official homepage says the Mac app is available now. It creates/edits floor plans, lays out furniture, checks dimensions/clearances, renders, and produces purchasable specifications. Only IKEA and Wayfair are described as live; the remaining retailer logos are explicitly planned integrations. This materially narrows the broad impression created by the sourcing workflow page.

Pricing page: free planning uses the customer's own AI connection; real-store sourcing and renders start at Pro $20/month or $17/month billed yearly. Pro includes 25 renders; Max tiers are $100 and $200/month for greater volume. Studio starts at $30/seat/month with two seats. Provider AI usage is additional according to the site's description; this memo does not validate its connection/billing mechanics.

Implication: practical fit and budget claims already exist at a low entry price, but broader supplier coverage and individual substitution quality remain testable differentiation candidates. Validate the actual fit checks and stock claims.

Sources: https://decato.ai/ ; https://decato.ai/pricing ; https://decato.ai/workflows/furniture-sourcing (undated; accessed 2026-09-13).

### Complecta AI — increasingly dealer/showroom-led specification

Its current page names dealers/multibrand showrooms as primary and individual interior designers as secondary. Workflow: render detection, visual/semantic/size matching, brand/budget/delivery-country filters, variant selection and structured specifications. A public app link exists; no access was attempted. Displayed tiers are Entry €39/user/month, Pro Studio €149/user/month and Enterprise from €899/month, plus AI tokens and brand limits/activation charges. Billing toggle presentation should be checked before purchase. Total catalog coverage was not verified.

Important contradiction: hero offers two free projects without a card; pricing section offers a free first month with a card. Neither should be represented as a confirmed trial contract. Traceability, country filters and editable decisions are already marketed.

Implication: country-aware matching is contested, and B2B supplier-funded distribution may matter. Do not equate a delivery-country filter with verified stock, shipping cost or deadline arrival.

Source: https://complecta.ai/ (undated; accessed 2026-09-13).

The page says Catalog is included in the subscription without an extra charge. Hero counts are 500,000+ products/250+ brands; FAQ still says 250,000+/130+. A second displayed price set is $79/$89/$119; the readable extract does not explicitly associate its cadence, so do not label it without checking. Account setup is marked mandatory; total onboarding cost was not established. Catalog and AI rendering appear across the tiers.

Commercial implication: existing customers face an incremental sourcing-feature price near zero, with established project context. A standalone app has to save enough additional work to justify another tool.

Sources: https://www.studiodesigner.com/features/catalog-for-interior-designers/ ; https://www.studiodesigner.com/pricing/ (undated; accessed 2026-09-13).

## Three testable entry hypotheses

These are research directions, not product commitments or established unmet needs.

### 1. Deadline-driven replacement sourcing for small residential studios

Hypothesis: a recurring urgent task—an approved item is unavailable or fails an exact constraint—has greater willingness to pay than broad inspiration search. Start with a small geography/category/supplier scope where variant, quantity, stock and destination can actually be checked. Deliver a few acceptable substitutes and a clear record of remaining checks.

Test: obtain 15–20 recent real replacement briefs from 8–12 studios, then compare a concierge-assisted approach with each studio's normal process, a free search baseline and available professional tools. Measure accepted replacements, total elapsed time, human verification time and downstream reversals. Charge for a subsequent real assignment, subject to a separately agreed pilot; verbal enthusiasm is insufficient.

Disconfirmation: urgent events are too rare; trusted reps outperform the service; location/quantity evidence needs too much manual work; results converge with existing tools; or users praise it but do not pay/reuse. Vendor access and service cost may be the business bottleneck, not AI quality.

### 2. Evidence-based review of a designer's existing shortlist

Hypothesis: designers value keeping control of aesthetic selection while delegating the tedious verification of exact variants, measurements, exclusions, totals and sourcing uncertainty. This could enter as a narrow review service or add-on rather than replacing discovery.

Test: blind-review real shortlists with intentionally seeded and naturally occurring issues. Separate objective fit facts from subjective compatibility judgments. Measure critical issues caught, false alarms, time saved, unresolved items and whether users change a decision. Test transfer into the firm's existing system without retyping. Proposed success criteria should be agreed before the pilot, not chosen after results.

Disconfirmation: current clipping/procurement tools already catch the relevant errors; verification does not change decisions; users still re-check everything; evidence access is inadequate; or an incumbent can satisfy the task within the same subscription. SPEKD's published cited/caveated answers show this is contested territory.

### 3. Specialized cross-supplier substitutions that preserve design intent

Hypothesis: a focused professional niche values alternatives that preserve nuanced visual relationships while honoring exclusions and practical limits, beyond nearest-image matching. Select the niche from interviews—do not presume residential, luxury or contract designers have the same needs. Keep the designer as final judge of subjective quality.

Test: ask professionals to state both accepted and rejected examples plus reasons; blind-rate alternatives from their process, specialist competitors and a prototype/concierge service. Measure viable contenders, distinctiveness, reject/revise cycles and time to client approval. Require repeated paid use across a project cycle.

Disconfirmation: vague taste criteria cause irreducible disagreement; designers prefer browsing; unique suppliers have inaccessible data; Hanei/Vendora/SPEKD/Fringe or the incumbent already perform adequately; or only novelty improves while actual purchase decisions do not.

## Recommended sequence before design commitment

1. Interview and observe professional studios using recent projects, including rejected options and invoices/orders where voluntarily shared. Distinguish principal buyer, sourcing employee and end client.
2. Establish frequency and business cost of the hardest sourcing tasks. Separate discovery, verification, approval and procurement effort. Do not assume more search time automatically means more willingness to pay.
3. Run controlled comparisons with identical briefs and source checks. Include at least one free tool and the customer's incumbent. Track evidence freshness, exact variant, quantity, destination, total cost and uncertainty separately from visual relevance.
4. Test a paid manual/concierge delivery before deciding whether to build a standalone product, add-on or supplier-integrated service.
5. Keep DIY as a later, separate validation track. Professional repeat use, trade relationships and client-facing deliverables are different from episodic personal shopping; a shared retrieval engine does not prove a shared business model.

Research status: complete for public-source competitive discovery and positioning. Unresolved: actual product performance, gated availability, paying adoption/retention, exact plan entitlements, vendor-data rights, coverage freshness, distribution economics and customer willingness to pay.

## Latest direction: persistent research across new, used, local and unique inventory

This update supersedes any impression above that the vision is limited to fixed catalogs or approved-brand APIs. The user clarified a general-purpose research/sourcing agent **within furniture and interiors**, able to persist over time and explore creative acquisition routes including Facebook Marketplace, Craigslist, eBay, secondhand/local inventory and unique pieces. Initial customers remain professionals; DIY is later. The narrow tests above are possible entry experiments, not intended limits on that broader product vision.

### The competitive set changes materially

- **Aggario:** publicly describes an AI buying agent with natural-language watches, continuous scanning of connected sources, valuation/match scores and drafted offers. It is beta. Crucially, its own source table says eBay and Etsy are credential-dependent beta; Facebook Marketplace and OfferUp are import-only today; Craigslist is also import-only. Thus its headline is a conceptual competitor, not proof of fully automated cross-marketplace coverage. No pricing confirmed. Source: https://aggario.com/ (undated; accessed 2026-09-13).
- **Swoopa:** markets continuous cross-marketplace monitoring, alerts, positive/negative keyword filtering, AI value assessment and price-drop signals. Current main homepage emphasizes vehicle acquisition; help materials also describe general-item searches. It is adjacent to furniture hunting, not verified as furniture-specialist reasoning. Published monthly plans show Go $47, Turbo $144 and Nitro $352, with lower committed rates. Other landing pages contain inconsistent trials/search counts; verify before relying on exact entitlements. Claimed alert speed, coverage, access permissions and value estimates were not independently tested. Sources: https://getswoopa.com/ ; https://getswoopa.com/subscription-options/ ; https://getswoopa.com/help-center/ (undated; accessed 2026-09-13).
- **SearchTempest:** longstanding classifieds search aggregation, with official description covering Facebook Marketplace, Craigslist and eBay. It describes Google Custom Search for Craigslist and APIs for eBay. Public page requires JavaScript for functional search. This establishes an aggregation baseline, not comprehensive live inventory or autonomous research. Source: https://www.searchtempest.com/about (undated; accessed 2026-09-13).
- **Native alerts already solve part of persistence:** eBay saved searches send new-listing emails and allow filters; Craigslist offers saved-search emails, including matching renewed/edited posts, with adaptive frequency; Chairish supports followed filtered searches with new-item and sale notifications. Persistent notification alone is not unique. Sources: https://www.ebay.com/help/default/default/saved-searches?id=4051 ; https://www.craigslist.org/about/help/account/features/alerts ; https://support.chairish.com/hc/en-us/articles/115006618048-My-Saved-Searches (undated; accessed 2026-09-13).

The candidate differentiation is consequently **adaptive, project-aware research across fragmented sources**, with useful judgment and follow-through over the life of a brief. That differs in ambition from static keyword alerts, but market demand and practical superiority are still unproven. The source-discovery breadth should be an explicit evaluation dimension, not an assumed consequence of calling something an agent.

### Revised highest-priority entry hypothesis

A potentially suitable initial customer is a professional residential designer who already mixes vintage/used statement pieces with new furniture and repeatedly hunts for a particular piece over days or weeks. Hypothesis: they would pay to delegate that persistent hunt while keeping aesthetic selection and purchasing decisions. This is more aligned with the user's clarified vision than a fixed-catalog replacement finder. Urgent replacement remains a separate test segment, not the prescribed whole business.

A possible pilot: one metropolitan area, several active project briefs, a four-week observation window, and an intentionally mixed source set (new retail, specialist vintage dealers, permitted marketplace access, existing native alerts and designer-shared listings). The bounded pilot tests the broad research behavior without pretending every source is already accessible. A brief can expand intelligently to different names, eras, materials, neighboring locations, resale routes or restoration options when the designer permits those tradeoffs. Each recommendation should show why it fits, the source, when observed, what is still unknown and the action needed before it is lost.

Compare against the designer's normal multi-tab/rep process **and a well-configured set of native saved alerts**, plus Swoopa/SearchTempest where usable. Measure useful opportunities uniquely found, time to first viable candidate, alert precision, time saved, stale/sold alerts, duplicate listings, client approval speed, actual acquired pieces and total acquisition cost including delivery/restoration. A larger result count is not a success measure.

Disconfirmation includes: native alerts already surface the same useful pieces; designers enjoy and will not delegate the hunt; attractive one-off finds disappear before client approval; pickup or restoration eliminates apparent savings; uncertain provenance/condition blocks professional use; the agent cannot obtain adequate source access; or manual operations destroy gross margin. The test should reveal which problem matters before choosing interface or infrastructure.

### Acquisition and economic tests suggested by this direction

- Recruit a small initial cohort through professional vintage-sourcing communities, boutique studios and antique/dealer relationships; first verify such channels can introduce relevant designers rather than assuming cheap distribution. No outreach has been performed.
- Offer a defined paid project hunt or subscription for a limited number of active briefs as experiments. Track renewal and actual additional project usage, not sign-ups. A fixed outcome guarantee is unsuitable before understanding sourcing uncertainty.
- Measure research cost per active brief-day, source access cost, human verification minutes, alert-to-acquisition rate, days a brief remains open and refunds/support. Continuing forever for a flat low price can be uneconomic even when model calls are cheap.
- Distinguish access capability, lawful/contractual permissions and operational reliability source by source. Aggario's explicit import-only limitations are evidence that public product ambition can greatly exceed live integrations. This memo has not established access rights for the proposed app; the parent research covers source feasibility.

Updated status: complete for this public-source competitor pass, including the clarified broader sourcing vision. The report provides hypotheses and benchmark candidates, not a validated market opportunity or proven complete source coverage.

### Preserve the full design experience

The initial research entry should be described as an **ongoing design brief around the client's existing room**, not a race to ship a narrow search utility or take market share quickly. Existing furniture, photographs, spatial limits, aesthetic intent, budget allocation and the designer's evolving judgment belong to the context. The persistent hunt is one capability within that larger experience: it revisits unresolved needs, surfaces worthwhile unexpected finds and explains the tradeoffs in the room. Validation exercises above are ways to learn whether that experience helps professionals, not a commitment to strip the final vision down to an urgent-replacement tool. Rescue briefs can provide useful measurable examples but must not replace the user's broader design ambition.
